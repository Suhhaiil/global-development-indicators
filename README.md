# Global Development Indicators: A Comparative Analysis of 15 Economies, 2000–2024

## Project Overview

This project examines economic and demographic indicators across 15 economies between 2000 and 2024 using data from the World Bank's World Development Indicators database.

Coming from a Political Science background, I wanted to explore how quantitative data analysis can help compare countries and understand differences in economic conditions. The project combines data cleaning, SQL queries, Python analysis and an interactive Power BI dashboard.

## Research Objectives

- Compare economic and demographic indicators across 15 economies.
- Examine historical trends between 2000 and 2024.
- Identify differences in GDP per capita, inflation, unemployment and population.
- Apply data-analysis tools to organise, analyse and communicate findings.

## Indicators Analysed

| Indicator | Description |
|---|---|
| GDP per capita | GDP per capita in current US dollars |
| Inflation | Annual percentage change in consumer prices |
| Unemployment | Modelled ILO estimate of unemployment as a percentage of the labour force |
| Population | Total population |

## Countries Covered

Brazil, China, France, Germany, India, Indonesia, Ireland, Italy, Japan, the Netherlands, the Russian Federation, South Africa, Spain, the United Kingdom and the United States.

## Tools Used

- **Microsoft Excel:** Data organisation, cleaning and quality checks
- **SQL:** Data queries, country comparisons and summary calculations
- **Python:** Data inspection, analysis and visualisation using pandas
- **Power BI:** Interactive dashboard and historical trend visualisations

## Key Findings

- GDP per capita varied substantially across the selected economies in 2024.
- Inflation and unemployment estimates differed considerably between countries.
- India and China had the largest populations in the selected group.
- Historical changes in nominal GDP per capita varied across the study period.

These findings are descriptive. GDP per capita in current US dollars is affected by price changes and exchange rates and should not be interpreted as a measure of real economic growth.

## Limitations

This project is descriptive and covers a selected group of economies. GDP per capita is measured in current US dollars and is not adjusted for inflation or purchasing-power differences. National averages also do not capture income inequality within countries.

## Future Work

The analysis could be extended by incorporating indicators related to education, poverty, income inequality and governance to develop a broader understanding of development outcomes.
